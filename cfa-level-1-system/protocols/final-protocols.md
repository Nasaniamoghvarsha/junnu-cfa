## 🎯 EXAM DAY PROTOCOL

### Morning Routine
- Wake up early. Do a light stretch or workout to get blood flowing.
- Eat a balanced breakfast (protein + complex carbs). Avoid sugar crashes. Example: oatmeal and eggs.
- Arrive at the test center 45 minutes before your scheduled time.
- Use the restroom right before checking in.

### Session 1 Strategy (Morning)
- **Structure**: 135 minutes, 90 questions. Covers Ethics, Quant, Economics, FSA.
- **Pacing**: **90 seconds per question.**
- **The "Three-Pass" Approach**:
  1. **First Pass**: Answer all questions you know immediately and can solve quickly. (Target: 0-60 secs/question). Flag anything you aren't 100% sure about or that requires a long calculation.
  2. **Second Pass**: Go back to the flagged questions that you know how to do but require more time/calculation. (Target: 60-120 secs/question).
  3. **Third Pass**: Address the hardest questions you flagged. Use process of elimination. If you still don't know, GUESS. **Never leave a question blank.**

### The Break
- Take the full optional break.
- Eat a light snack (banana, protein bar, water). Avoid heavy meals that cause a food coma.
- DO NOT check your phone or look up answers to morning questions. It will only cause anxiety.
- Stretch your legs, use the restroom, clear your head.

### Session 2 Strategy (Afternoon)
- **Structure**: 135 minutes, 90 questions. Covers Corporate Issuers, Equity, Fixed Income, Derivatives, Alt Investments, Portfolio Management.
- **Pacing**: Maintain the 90 seconds per question rule. Fatigue will set in here; rely on your Week 2 Mock Intensive stamina.
- Stick to the Three-Pass Approach. Do not let one difficult Fixed Income calculation derail your timing.

### Question Approach Tactics
1. **Read the LAST sentence first**: Know exactly what they are asking before reading the data.
2. **Read the full stem carefully**: Pay attention to dates, units, and specific qualifiers.
3. **Identify the concept/Learning Outcome**: What is CFAI testing here?
4. **Anticipate the answer**: Try to solve or conceptualize the answer before looking at the options A, B, C.
5. **Process of Elimination**: Cross out the obvious wrong answer. Often, two answers are conceptually similar, and one is the outlier.
6. **Watch out for "LEAST likely" or "MOST accurate"**: Underline these words mentally.
7. **First instinct is usually right**: Do not change answers unless you find concrete evidence you misread the question.

## ☠️ DANGER ZONES TO AVOID (The "Do Nots")

1. **The "Least Likely" Trap**: CFAI loves asking what is "least likely" or "most inaccurate." If you are moving too fast, you will select the first true statement you see. **Read carefully.**
2. **Formula Blindness**: Memorizing a formula without understanding the inputs. (e.g., Using book value instead of market value for WACC).
3. **The Economics Rabbit Hole**: Spending 15 hours trying to master IS-LM curves or complex microeconomics when the whole subject is only 6-9%. Focus on FX and high-yield macro.
4. **Over-studying on Day 1**: Cramming the day before the exam will only exhaust your brain. You need cognitive freshness more than you need to review one last minor concept.
5. **Ignoring IFRS vs. US GAAP Differences**: This is tested heavily in FSA. Create a specific comparison sheet and memorize the major differences (e.g., LIFO allowed in GAAP, banned in IFRS; interest paid classification).
6. **Changing Answers**: Second-guessing yourself usually leads to changing a correct answer to a wrong one. Only change it if you have a "lightbulb" moment of realizing a factual error.
7. **Getting Stuck**: Spending 5 minutes on a single calculation question. If you don't know it, guess, flag it, and move on. All questions are worth the exact same points. Don't sacrifice 3 easy questions for 1 hard one.

## 🔥 HIGH-VALUE EXAM REMINDERS & CHEAT SHEET

### General Math & Concepts
- **CFA FX Convention**: EUR/USD = 1.10 means 1 EUR = 1.10 USD. (Base currency is the first one). Up/Up, Down/Down for Bid/Ask spreads.
- **NPV vs. IRR**: NPV always beats IRR for mutually exclusive projects. NPV assumes reinvestment at cost of capital; IRR assumes reinvestment at IRR.
- **Gordon Growth Model**: Use D₁ (expected next dividend), not D₀ (just paid). D₁ = D₀ * (1+g).
- **Bond Prices**: Price and yield move in OPPOSITE directions. Convexity is a good thing for bondholders.
- **WACC**: Uses MARKET values of debt and equity, not book values.
- **Callable Bonds**: OAS < Z-spread. Option cost = Z-spread - OAS.
- **DuPont Analysis**: ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Financial Leverage.
- **Normal Distribution**: 68% within 1 SD, 95% within 2 SD, 99% within 3 SD.

### Ethics Quick Hits
- **Strictest law/standard always applies**: If local law is stricter than CFA, follow local law. If CFA is stricter, follow CFA.
- **Disclosure does not equal prohibition**: You can often do things (like accept a gift) as long as you disclose it to all parties (usually employer in writing).
- **Fair dealing does not mean equal dealing**: You can offer premium service levels, provided they are available to everyone and not at the expense of other clients.
- **Mosaic Theory**: Public information + non-material nonpublic information = OK to trade on.
- **GIPS**: Applies on a firm-wide basis. Only investment management firms can claim compliance. Software cannot be "GIPS compliant."
