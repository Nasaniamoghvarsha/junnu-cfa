# CFA Level I — Ethics Question Bank

## Standard I: Professionalism

---

### Q-ETH-0001 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Strictest Law Apply

**Question:**
Maria Chen, CFA, works as an investment analyst in Country A, where securities laws prohibit trading on material nonpublic information but allow analysts to accept modest gifts from companies they cover. According to the CFA Institute Standards of Professional Conduct, which of the following is most appropriate for Chen?
A) Follow Country A's laws regarding gifts since they are more permissive
B) Follow only CFA Institute Standards since they are globally recognized
C) Follow CFA Institute Standards regarding gifts because they are stricter than Country A's laws on this matter


**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Under Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law, members must comply with the stricter of applicable laws, rules, or CFA Institute Standards. If Country A's laws are more permissive on gifts than the CFA Standards (which require independence and objectivity), Chen must follow the stricter CFA Standards. The fundamental principle is "strictest law/standard applies."

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — the "strictest" rule applies, not simply following local law when it's weaker
- B: Incorrect — Standards don't override stricter local laws; the strictest standard applies

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-01-LO01 (Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law)
**Related Concepts:** Independence, hierarchy of rules
**Common Misconception:** Assuming local law always prevails

### Q-ETH-0002 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Gift Threshold

**Question:**
Thomas Park, CFA, receives a bottle of wine worth approximately $150 from a company he covers after publishing a favorable research report. Park did not know the gift was coming. According to the CFA Institute Standards, what is Park's most appropriate action?
A) Keep the wine since he didn't expect it and the report was already published
B) Return the wine and report the incident to his compliance department
C) Keep the wine but disclose it to his employer


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Under Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity, members must not accept gifts that could reasonably be expected to compromise their independence. A $150 gift is not token and could create the appearance of influence. Returning the gift and reporting to compliance protects independence. Simply keeping it — even with disclosure — could reasonably be seen as compromising objectivity.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — timing of the gift relative to the report doesn't eliminate the appearance issue
- B: Partially correct but incomplete — returning alone without reporting leaves no record
- C: Incorrect — disclosure doesn't cure a gift that reasonably appears to compromise independence

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-01-LO02 (Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity)
**Related Concepts:** Gift policy, token items
**Common Misconception:** That disclosure cures all gift-related conflicts

### Q-ETH-0003 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Plagiarism vs. Attribution

**Question:**
An analyst includes a chart from a well-known industry publication in her research report. She cites the source in a footnote but reproduces the chart without permission. Does this most likely violate Standard I(C) Misrepresentation?
A) No, because proper citation was provided
B) No, because industry data is considered public domain
C) Yes, because reproducing without permission is plagiarism regardless of citation


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Standard I(C) prohibits misrepresentation, including plagiarism. However, providing proper attribution/citation typically satisfies the Standard's requirements. The Standard does not require obtaining permission from the original source — attribution is sufficient to avoid misrepresentation. Copyright law is a separate legal matter, but for CFA Standards purposes, proper citation addresses the misrepresentation concern.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — the Standard doesn't distinguish "public domain" material; attribution is still needed
- C: Incorrect — plagiarism under the Standards is about failing to attribute, not about permission

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-01-LO03 (Standard I(C) Misrepresentation)
**Related Concepts:** Plagiarism, attribution, copyright distinction
**Common Misconception:** Confusing plagiarism (failure to attribute) with copyright infringement

### Q-ETH-0004 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: "Most Likely" Question | Trap: Personal vs. Professional Conduct

**Question:**
A CFA charterholder is convicted of drunk driving (a misdemeanor) in his personal time. According to Standard I(D) Misconduct, does this most likely constitute a violation?
A) Yes, because any criminal conviction reflects poorly on the profession
B) Yes, because charterholders must maintain high standards at all times
C) No, because the conduct was in his personal time and unrelated to professional activities


**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Standard I(D) concerns professional misconduct — acts involving dishonesty, fraud, or deceit, or acts that reflect adversely on professional reputation. A single DUI misdemeanor, while serious, may not necessarily reflect on professional honesty or integrity in a way that triggers Standard I(D). The Standard is not a blanket morality clause for all personal conduct.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — not ALL criminal convictions automatically violate the Standard
- B: Incorrect — the Standard focuses on professional integrity, not all personal behavior

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-01-LO04 (Standard I(D) Misconduct)
**Related Concepts:** Professional vs. personal conduct
**Common Misconception:** That any bad behavior violates the Standards

### Q-ETH-0005 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Multiple Standards Application

**Question:**
A portfolio manager learns from a friend who works at a regulatory agency that a major enforcement action will be announced against Company X next week. The manager immediately sells all client positions in Company X. Which Standard(s) has the manager most likely violated?
A) Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information only
B) Standard II(A) and Standard III(B) Fair Dealing
C) Standard II(A) and Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The manager traded on material nonpublic information, violating Standard II(A). There's no evidence of unfair dealing (all clients were treated the same) or disloyalty (selling to protect clients from losses is arguably in their interest). The violation is specifically trading on inside information, regardless of the direction of the trade.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — no evidence of unequal treatment among clients
- C: Incorrect — protecting clients from losses could be seen as loyal/prudent

**LO Reference:** ETH-03-01-LO01 (Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information)
**Related Concepts:** MNPI, insider trading
**Common Misconception:** That trading on MNPI is only illegal when buying (selling is also a violation)

### Q-ETH-0006 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Mosaic Theory

**Question:**
An analyst gathers information from multiple public sources about a company. She also speaks with the company's former employees (who are not bound by confidentiality) about industry trends. Combining all this information, she concludes the company's earnings will decline and recommends selling. Has she most likely violated Standard II(A)?
A) Yes, because speaking with former employees is improper
B) Yes, because the conclusion was based on nonpublic information
C) No, because all information used was either public or non-material nonpublic


**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** This is a classic mosaic theory scenario. The analyst combined public information with non-material nonpublic information (general industry trends from former employees, not specific confidential data) to reach her own conclusion. The mosaic theory permits using non-material nonpublic information as part of analysis, as long as you don't trade on material nonpublic information.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — speaking with former employees about non-confidential matters is permissible
- B: Incorrect — the key is whether the nonpublic information was MATERIAL, not just nonpublic

**LO Reference:** ETH-03-01-LO01 (Standard II(A) MNPI — Mosaic Theory)
**Related Concepts:** Mosaic theory, materiality
**Common Misconception:** That any nonpublic information cannot be used

### Q-ETH-0007 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: "Least Likely" Question | Trap: Market Manipulation Definition

**Question:**
Which of the following is least likely to violate Standard II(B) Market Manipulation?
A) Placing large buy orders near market close to boost the reported closing price
B) Executing a large block trade at a price negotiated privately between buyer and seller
C) Simultaneously buying and selling the same security to create an appearance of high trading volume


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** A privately negotiated block trade is a legitimate transaction — it does not distort prices or create artificial volume. The other three options are classic examples of market manipulation: (A) is marking the close, (B) is spreading false information, and (C) is wash trading.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: This IS manipulation — "marking the close"
- B: This IS manipulation — information-based manipulation
- C: This IS manipulation — wash trading to create false volume

**LO Reference:** ETH-03-01-LO02 (Standard II(B) Market Manipulation)
**Related Concepts:** Price distortion, artificial volume
**Common Misconception:** Not all large trades are manipulative

### Q-ETH-0008 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Fair Dealing vs. Suitability

**Question:**
A) portfolio manager receives an IPO allocation of 1,000 shares. She has three clients for whom the IPO would be suitable. Client A has $10M in assets, Client
B) has $5M, and Client
C) has $1M. The manager allocates 333 shares to each client. Does this most likely comply with Standard III(B) Fair Dealing?

A) Yes, because all suitable clients received equal allocation
B) No, because allocation should be proportional to account size
C) No, because the manager should have allocated only to the largest client


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Fair dealing means all clients must be treated fairly, but "fair" doesn't necessarily mean "equal." A pro-rata allocation based on account size (500 to A, 250 to B, 100 to C, or similar proportionality) is the standard practice. Equal allocation when account sizes differ dramatically is not fair — the smallest client gets a disproportionately large allocation.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — fair ≠ equal; allocation should reflect account size
- C: Incorrect — all suitable clients should receive allocation

**LO Reference:** ETH-04-01-LO02 (Standard III(B) Fair Dealing)
**Related Concepts:** IPO allocation, pro-rata
**Common Misconception:** That "fair dealing" means equal treatment

### Q-ETH-0009 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Suitability

**Question:**
An investment advisor recommends a high-risk, leveraged ETF to an 85-year-old client whose primary objective is capital preservation and income. The advisor believes the ETF will generate superior returns. Has the advisor most likely violated Standard III(C) Suitability?
A) Yes, because the recommendation does not match the client's stated objectives
B) No, because the advisor believes the investment will perform well
C) No, as long as the advisor disclosed the risks of the ETF


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Standard III(C) requires that recommendations be suitable based on the client's investment objectives, risk tolerance, and constraints. A high-risk leveraged investment is fundamentally unsuitable for a client with capital preservation as the primary objective, regardless of the advisor's performance expectations or risk disclosures.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — the advisor's belief doesn't override suitability requirements
- C: Incorrect — disclosure of risks doesn't make an unsuitable investment suitable

**LO Reference:** ETH-04-01-LO03 (Standard III(C) Suitability)
**Related Concepts:** IPS, risk-return profile, client objectives
**Common Misconception:** That disclosure cures suitability problems

### Q-ETH-0010 | Difficulty: 4 | Time: 120s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Multiple Standards Interaction

**Question:**
A CFA charterholder is leaving her current employer to start her own firm. Before resigning, she downloads client contact information, performance records, and proprietary investment models from her employer's database. She did not have a non-compete agreement but signed a confidentiality agreement. Which Standards has she most likely violated?
A) Standard IV(A) Loyalty to Employer only
B) Standard IV(A) and Standard III(E) Preservation of Confidentiality
C) Standard IV(A) and Standard V(C) Record Retention


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Taking employer property (client lists, proprietary models) before leaving violates Standard IV(A) Loyalty to Employer — she deprived the employer of its property. The confidentiality agreement is an employment contract matter. Standard III(E) concerns client confidentiality, not employer confidentiality. Standard V(C) concerns maintaining records, not taking them. Primary violation is loyalty to employer.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — client performance records are not the same as confidential client information
- C: Incorrect — V(C) is about maintaining adequate records, not misappropriating employer records

**LO Reference:** ETH-05-01-LO01 (Standard IV(A) Loyalty)
**Related Concepts:** Departing employees, employer property
**Common Misconception:** Assuming all confidentiality breaches trigger multiple Standards

### Q-ETH-0011 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Diligence and Reasonable Basis

**Question:**
An analyst issues a "Strong Buy" recommendation on a stock after reading the company's annual report and speaking with management at an investor conference. The analyst did not build a financial model or verify management's claims with independent sources. The stock subsequently declines 60%. Has the analyst most likely violated Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis?
A) Yes, because the analysis was insufficient to support a "Strong Buy" rating
B) No, because reading the annual report and speaking with management constitutes reasonable diligence
C) Yes, because the stock declined, proving the recommendation was wrong


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Standard V(A) requires exercise of diligence and thoroughness in developing recommendations. For a "Strong Buy" rating (a high-conviction recommendation), merely reading the annual report and attending a conference is unlikely to constitute sufficient research. The analyst should have conducted more thorough analysis including independent verification. The outcome (stock decline) is evidence but not the test — the test is whether the process was diligent.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — this level of analysis is unlikely sufficient for a high-conviction recommendation
- C: Incorrect — the Standard tests process, not outcomes; bad outcomes don't equal violations

**LO Reference:** ETH-06-01-LO01 (Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis)
**Related Concepts:** Research process, due diligence, independent verification
**Common Misconception:** That bad outcomes mean bad process

### Q-ETH-0012 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Fact vs. Opinion

**Question:**
A research report states: "Company X will definitely double its earnings next year because its new CEO is a proven leader." Which Standard is most likely violated?
A) Standard V(B) Communication with Clients — failure to distinguish fact from opinion
B) Standard V(A) Diligence and Reasonable Basis — insufficient research
C) Standard I(C) Misrepresentation — false statement about future earnings


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The statement presents a prediction ("will definitely double") as if it were a fact rather than an opinion. Standard V(B) requires analysts to distinguish between fact and opinion in communications. The report should state something like "In my opinion, earnings could increase substantially given the new CEO's track record." The certainty language is the violation.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Possible but secondary — the primary violation is the presentation, not the research process
- C: Overstated — predicting earnings growth is not necessarily misrepresentation

**LO Reference:** ETH-06-01-LO02 (Standard V(B) Communication with Clients)
**Related Concepts:** Fact vs. opinion, forward-looking statements
**Common Misconception:** That all predictions are opinions and therefore protected

### Q-ETH-0013 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Disclosure vs. Prohibition

**Question:**
A CFA charterholder recommends a local wealth management firm to several of her clients and receives a referral fee of $500 per client. She does not tell her clients or employer about this arrangement. Which Standard has she most likely violated?
A) Standard VI(C) Referral Fees — failure to disclose
B) Standard IV(B) Additional Compensation — failure to get employer consent
C) Both Standard VI(C) and Standard IV(B)


**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The charterholder violated Standard VI(C) by failing to disclose the referral fees to clients. She also likely violated Standard IV(B) by accepting additional compensation that could conflict with employer interests without written consent from all parties. Both Standards require disclosure; referral fees are not prohibited — they must be disclosed.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Partially correct but incomplete
- B: Partially correct but incomplete

**LO Reference:** ETH-07-01-LO03 (Standard VI(C) Referral Fees), ETH-05-01-LO02 (Standard IV(B))
**Related Concepts:** Disclosure requirements, conflicts of interest
**Common Misconception:** That referral fees are prohibited

### Q-ETH-0014 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Priority of Transactions

**Question:**
A portfolio manager receives a client order to buy 10,000 shares of XYZ Corp. Before executing the client order, the manager buys 1,000 shares for her personal account at the same price. The client order is then executed. The stock subsequently rises. Has the manager most likely violated Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions?
A) Yes, because client transactions must take priority over personal transactions
B) No, because the small personal trade did not affect the client's execution price
C) Yes, but only if the manager's trade caused the client to pay a higher price


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Standard VI(B) is absolute — client transactions must take priority over personal transactions. The size of the trade, the price impact, or whether it was executed the same day are all irrelevant. The violation is trading ahead of the client, regardless of outcome. This is a strict priority rule.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — price impact is irrelevant to the priority rule
- C: Incorrect — the violation occurs from the act of trading ahead, not from the price effect

**LO Reference:** ETH-07-01-LO02 (Standard VI(B) Priority of Transactions)
**Related Concepts:** Front-running, client priority
**Common Misconception:** That small trades ahead of clients are acceptable

### Q-ETH-0015 | Difficulty: 4 | Time: 120s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: GIPS Compliance

**Question:**
A small investment firm manages $50 million in assets and wants to claim compliance with GIPS. The firm manages both discretionary and non-discretionary portfolios. Which of the following is most accurate regarding GIPS compliance?
A) The firm must include all fee-paying discretionary portfolios in at least one composite
B) The firm can claim GIPS compliance for only certain asset classes it manages
C) The firm is too small to claim GIPS compliance


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** GIPS compliance is firm-wide, meaning all actual, fee-paying, discretionary portfolios must be included in at least one composite. There is no minimum size requirement for GIPS compliance. Non-discretionary portfolios are excluded from composites. A firm cannot claim compliance for only part of its business — it's all or nothing.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — compliance is firm-wide, not selective by asset class
- C: Incorrect — GIPS has no minimum firm size requirement

**LO Reference:** ETH-09-01-LO02 (GIPS Compliance Requirements)
**Related Concepts:** Firm-wide compliance, composites, discretionary portfolios
**Common Misconception:** That small firms can't claim GIPS compliance

### Q-ETH-0016 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Supervisor Responsibility

**Question:**
Sarah Chen, CFA, is a senior portfolio manager supervising a team of three junior analysts. One junior analyst, without Chen's knowledge, publishes a research report that contains material copied from another firm's report without attribution. Under Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors, has Chen violated the Standard?
A) Yes, because supervisors are responsible for detecting and preventing violations
B) No, because Chen had no knowledge of the violation
C) Yes, but only if Chen failed to have reasonable compliance procedures in place


**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Standard IV(C) requires supervisors to make reasonable efforts to detect and prevent violations. The Standard does not impose strict liability — a supervisor violates the Standard only if they FAILED to establish reasonable compliance procedures or FAILED to enforce them. If Chen had reasonable procedures in place and the analyst circumvented them, Chen may not be in violation.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — the Standard requires "reasonable efforts," not absolute liability
- B: Incorrect — lack of knowledge doesn't automatically excuse if procedures were inadequate

**LO Reference:** ETH-05-01-LO03 (Standard IV(C) Responsibilities of Supervisors)
**Related Concepts:** Compliance procedures, supervisory responsibility
**Common Misconception:** That supervisors are strictly liable for all subordinate violations

### Q-ETH-0017 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: "Most Appropriate Action" | Trap: Standard Overlap

**Question:**
A CFA candidate posts the following on LinkedIn: "John Smith, CFA Level II Candidate." Has the candidate most likely violated Standard VII(B)?
A) Yes, because "CFA" must never be used as a noun or adjective
B) No, because the candidate accurately stated his candidacy status
C) Yes, because candidates cannot reference the CFA Program publicly


**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Standard VII(B) explicitly prohibits using "CFA" as a noun. The proper reference would be "John Smith has passed Level I of the CFA Program" or "John Smith is a Level II candidate in the CFA Program." The designation "CFA" (even when followed by "Level II Candidate") cannot be used as a noun or adjective.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — even if factually accurate, the FORMAT violates the Standard
- C: Incorrect — candidates CAN reference candidacy, but must do it properly

**LO Reference:** ETH-08-01-LO02 (Standard VII(B) Reference to CFA Institute)
**Related Concepts:** Proper usage of CFA marks
**Common Misconception:** That accurate factual references can't violate the Standard

### Q-ETH-0018 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Disclosure of Conflicts

**Question:**
An analyst holds shares in a company she covers. Her research report includes a "Buy" recommendation. At the end of the report, there is a small footnote stating: "The analyst holds a position in this security." Has the analyst complied with Standard VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts?
A) Yes, because the conflict was disclosed
B) No, because the disclosure should be prominent, not buried in a footnote
C) Yes, as long as the analyst's holding did not influence the recommendation


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Standard VI(A) requires "full and fair disclosure" of conflicts. A small footnote at the end of a report does not constitute prominent disclosure. The disclosure should be clear, noticeable, and placed where readers will actually see it — ideally at the beginning of the report or in a prominent disclosure section.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — mere disclosure is not sufficient; it must be meaningful disclosure
- C: Incorrect — the Standard requires DISCLOSURE regardless of actual influence

**LO Reference:** ETH-07-01-LO01 (Standard VI(A) Disclosure of Conflicts)
**Related Concepts:** Full and fair disclosure, materiality
**Common Misconception:** That any disclosure, however buried, satisfies the Standard

### Q-ETH-0019 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Performance Presentation

**Question:**
A firm's marketing material states: "Our Large Cap Strategy has outperformed the S&P 500 in 8 of the last 10 years." **Wrong Answer Analysis:**

- C: Incorrect — disclosure helps but may not cure fundamentally misleading presentation

**LO Reference:** ETH-04-01-LO04 (Standard III(D) Performance Presentation)
**Related Concepts:** Cherry-picking, survivorship bias
**Common Misconception:** That technically accurate statements can't be misleading

### Q-ETH-0021 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Directed Brokerage & Soft Dollars

**Question:**
An investment manager uses client brokerage commissions to pay for research reports from a broker-dealer that assist the manager in making investment decisions for all client accounts. Additionally, the manager uses soft dollars to purchase office furniture and computer hardware. According to Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care, which of the following best describes the manager's compliance status?
A) Compliant with all soft dollar uses because research benefits client accounts

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — office furniture and general hardware do not provide direct research assistance to clients.
- C: Incorrect — soft dollar research is permissible if it assists in the investment decision-making process for clients.

**LO Reference:** ETH-04-01-LO01 (Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care)
**Related Concepts:** Soft dollars, directed brokerage, fiduciary duty
**Common Misconception:** Believing all soft dollar usage is prohibited or that any overhead expense can be paid with soft dollars.

### Q-ETH-0022 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Block Trading & Allocation

**Question:**
A portfolio manager places a block trade to purchase shares of a promising IPO for several client accounts. The order is only partially filled. The manager allocates the filled portion pro-rata across all participating accounts based on order size, but excludes his own personal account that was included in the original block. Did the manager violate Standard III(B) Fair Dealing?
A) Yes, because personal accounts must receive identical pro-rata allocation in block orders

C) Yes, because pro-rata allocation is required whenever an order is partially filled

When a block trade is partially filled, excluding the manager's personal account to fully allocate available shares to clients is proper and compliant.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — client accounts must take priority over personal trading; personal accounts should not participate on equal footing when orders are partially filled.
- C: Incorrect — pro-rata allocation applies among client accounts, but client accounts precede personal accounts.

**LO Reference:** ETH-04-01-LO02 (Standard III(B) Fair Dealing)
**Related Concepts:** Trade allocation, partial fills, priority of transactions
**Common Misconception:** Assuming pro-rata rules require personal accounts to receive equal allocation during partial fills.

### Q-ETH-0024 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Simulated vs. Actual Performance

**Question:**
An investment advisory firm publishes a marketing brochure showing a 5-year annualized return of 18.5% for its growth strategy. The return figures are based on backtested model portfolio performance, but the brochure clearly labels them as "Simulated Model Returns" in a prominent footnote. Does this presentation comply with Standard III(D) Performance Presentation?
A) Yes, because clear disclosure of simulated performance eliminates misrepresentation
B) No, because simulated backtested performance can never be presented to prospective clients
C) Compliant only if actual client account performance is also presented alongside the model returns


**Correct Answer:** C
