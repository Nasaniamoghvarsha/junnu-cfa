### Q-ECO-0001 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Market Structure Features

**Question:**
Which market structure is characterized by many firms, differentiated products, low barriers to entry, and zero economic profit in the long run?
A) Perfect competition
B) Monopolistic competition
C) Oligopoly


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Monopolistic competition has: many firms, differentiated products (through branding, quality, location), low barriers to entry/exit, and zero economic profit in the long run (as new entrants erode profits). This differs from perfect competition (identical products) and oligopoly (few firms, high barriers).

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Perfect competition has IDENTICAL/homogeneous products, not differentiated
- C: Oligopoly has FEW firms and high barriers to entry

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO04
**Common Trap:** Confusing monopolistic competition (differentiated) with perfect competition (identical)

### Q-ECO-0002 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Elasticity & Revenue

**Question:**
A firm increases the price of its product by 5% and observes that quantity demanded decreases by 8%. Based on this, total revenue will most likely:
A) Increase
B) Decrease
C) Remain unchanged


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Elasticity = |%ΔQ / %ΔP| = |-8/5| = 1.6. Since |elasticity| > 1, demand is elastic. When demand is elastic, price increases lead to proportionally larger quantity decreases, causing total revenue to DECREASE. If demand were inelastic (|E| < 1), a price increase would raise total revenue.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: This would be true only for INELASTIC demand (< 1)
- C: This would be true only for UNIT elastic demand (|E| = 1)

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO01
**Formula:** E = (%ΔQ) / (%ΔP)
**Common Trap:** Forgetting the revenue-direction relationship

### Q-ECO-0003 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Shutdown vs. Breakeven

**Question:**
A firm sells its product for $25 per unit. Variable cost is $15 per unit and total fixed costs are $50,000. The firm currently produces and sells 4,000 units. Which of the following is most accurate?
A) The firm is breaking even
B) The firm should continue operating but is not breaking even
C) The firm is making a profit but below breakeven


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Revenue = 4,000 × $25 = $100,000. Variable cost = 4,000 × $15 = $60,000. Contribution margin = $40,000. Operating income = $40,000 - $50,000 = -$10,000 (loss). However, since Price ($25) > AVC ($15), the firm covers variable costs and contributes to fixed costs. Shutdown only if P < AVC. Breakeven: $50,000/($25-$15) = 5,000 units. The firm should continue operating but is below breakeven.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Breakeven is at 5,000 units, not 4,000
- B: Price ($25) > AVC ($15), so should NOT shut down
- C: The firm is making a LOSS, not a profit

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO03
**Common Trap:** Confusing shutdown point (P < AVC) with breakeven (P < ATC)

**Question:**
During the expansion phase of a business cycle, which of the following is most likely to occur?

A) Decrease the policy interest rate and buy government bonds

### Q-ECO-0007 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Fisher Effect

**Question:**
If the nominal interest rate is 7% and the expected inflation rate is 3%, the real interest rate is closest to:
A) 3.00%
B) 3.88%
C) 4.00%


**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Using the approximate Fisher equation: R_real ≈ R_nom - π^e = 7% - 3% = 4%. The exact formula: (1+R_nom) = (1+R_real)(1+π^e) → R_real = (1.07/1.03) - 1 = 3.88%. Since the question asks for "closest to," 4.00% is acceptable using the approximation.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Used inflation rate as the answer
- B: This is the exact Fisher calculation (3.88%)

**LO Reference:** ECO-04-01-LO02
**Formula:** R_nom = R_real + π^e
**Common Trap:** Confusing addition vs. subtraction in Fisher effect

### Q-ECO-0008 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Scenario Interpretation | Trap: Comparative Advantage

**Question:**
Country A can produce 10 units of wheat or 5 units of steel per labor hour. Country B can produce 8 units of wheat or 2 units of steel per labor hour. According to comparative advantage:
A) Country A should produce both goods because it has absolute advantage in both
B) Country A should specialize in steel and Country B in wheat
C) Country A should specialize in wheat and Country B in steel


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Compare opportunity costs:
- Country A: 1 wheat = 0.5 steel; 1 steel = 2 wheat
- Country B: 1 wheat = 0.25 steel; 1 steel = 4 wheat

Country B has lower opportunity cost for wheat (0.25 < 0.5 steel per wheat), so B has comparative advantage in wheat. Country A has lower opportunity cost for steel (2 < 4 wheat per steel), so A has comparative advantage in steel. A should specialize in steel, B in wheat.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Absolute advantage doesn't determine specialization — comparative advantage does
- C: This is backwards

**LO Reference:** ECO-05-01-LO01
**Common Trap:** Confusing absolute advantage (higher output) with comparative advantage (lower opportunity cost)

### Q-ECO-0015 | Difficulty: 4 | Time: 120s | Pattern: Integrated Question | Trap: Quantity Theory + Fisher

**Question:**
According to the quantity theory of money, if the money supply grows at 6%, real GDP grows at 2%, and the velocity of money is constant, the nominal interest rate is most likely:
A) 4%
B) Cannot be determined without knowing the real interest rate
C) Higher than the real interest rate


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** From MV = PY, with constant V: %ΔM + %ΔV = %ΔP + %ΔY. So %ΔP (inflation) = %ΔM - %ΔY = 6% - 2% = 4%. But the nominal interest rate = real rate + expected inflation (Fisher effect), and we don't know the real interest rate. So we only know inflation is 4%, but not the nominal rate.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: This is the inflation rate, not the nominal interest rate
- B: This is the money supply growth, not the nominal rate
- C: While technically true (R_nom = R_real + 4%, so R_nom > R_real), we still can't determine the exact nominal rate

**LO Reference:** ECO-04-01-LO02, ECO-04-01-LO04
**Formula:** MV = PY; Fisher: R_nom = R_real + π^e
**Common Trap:** Confusing inflation rate with nominal interest rate

### Q-ECO-0017 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Kinked Demand Curve Model

**Question:**
According to the kinked demand curve model of oligopoly, the demand curve facing an individual firm is highly ELASTIC for price increases above the prevailing price, but highly INELASTIC for price decreases below it. This model explains why:
A) Oligopoly firms continuously engage in price wars
B) Market prices in oligopoly markets tend to exhibit price rigidity
C) Marginal revenue is equal to price at all output levels


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** The **kinked demand curve model** assumes rival firms will NOT match a price increase (making demand above the current price elastic), but WILL match a price decrease (making demand below the current price inelastic). As a result, the marginal revenue curve has a vertical discontinuity ("gap") at the current output, causing the firm's optimal price to remain stable (rigid) even when marginal cost shifts within that gap.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — the model explains price STABILITY/rigidity, not price wars.
- C: Incorrect — MR is below price for any price-searcher firm.

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO06 (Oligopoly Kinked Demand Model)
**Related Concepts:** Kinked demand curve, price rigidity, MR gap
**Common Misconception:** Believing the kinked demand curve predicts frequent price changes.

### Q-ECO-0019 | Difficulty: 4 | Time: 120s | Pattern: Multi-Step Calculation | Trap: Fiscal Multiplier Formula

**Question:**
An economy has a marginal propensity to consume (MPC) of 0.80 and a marginal tax rate ($t$) of 25%. If the government increases government spending by $40 billion, the total estimated increase in aggregate demand (GDP) is:
A) $80 billion
B) $100 billion
C) $160 billion


**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Calculation of the Fiscal Expenditure Multiplier ($m_{\text{fiscal}}$):
$$m_{\text{fiscal}} = \frac{1}{1 - \text{MPC} \times (1 - t)}$$
$$m_{\text{fiscal}} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.80 \times (1 - 0.25)} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.80 \times 0.75} = \frac{1}{1 - 0.60} = \frac{1}{0.40} = 2.50$$
$$\Delta \text{GDP} = \Delta G \times m_{\text{fiscal}} = \$40\text{ billion} \times 2.50 = \$100\text{ billion}$$

**TI BA II Plus Keystrokes:**
- $1 - (0.80 \times 0.75) = 0.40$
- $1 / 0.40 = 2.50$
- $40 \times 2.50 = 100$

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — used incorrect tax multiplier calculation.
- C: Incorrect — ignored the tax rate ($t$) and used simple multiplier $\frac{1}{1 - 0.80} = 5.0 \implies 40 \times 5 = 200$.

**LO Reference:** ECO-04-01-LO03 (Fiscal Policy Multipliers)
**Related Concepts:** Fiscal multiplier, marginal propensity to consume, marginal tax rate
**Common Misconception:** Ignoring income tax rate $t$ in the fiscal multiplier formula denominator.

### Q-ECO-0021 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: CPI vs GDP Deflator

**Question:**
Which of the following statements comparing the Consumer Price Index (CPI) and the GDP Deflator is most accurate?

**LO Reference:** ECO-03-01-LO02 (Inflation Indicators)
**Related Concepts:** CPI, GDP Deflator, Laspeyres index, Paasche index, import substitution
