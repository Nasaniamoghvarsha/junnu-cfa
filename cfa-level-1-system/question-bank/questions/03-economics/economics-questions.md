### Q-ECO-0001 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Market Structure Features

**Question:**
Which market structure is characterized by many firms, differentiated products, low barriers to entry, and zero economic profit in the long run?

A) Perfect competition
B) Monopolistic competition
C) Oligopoly
D) Monopoly

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Monopolistic competition has: many firms, differentiated products (through branding, quality, location), low barriers to entry/exit, and zero economic profit in the long run (as new entrants erode profits). This differs from perfect competition (identical products) and oligopoly (few firms, high barriers).

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Perfect competition has IDENTICAL/homogeneous products, not differentiated
- C: Oligopoly has FEW firms and high barriers to entry
- D: Monopoly has ONE firm and very high barriers

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO04
**Common Trap:** Confusing monopolistic competition (differentiated) with perfect competition (identical)

### Q-ECO-0002 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Elasticity & Revenue

**Question:**
A firm increases the price of its product by 5% and observes that quantity demanded decreases by 8%. Based on this, total revenue will most likely:

A) Increase
B) Decrease
C) Remain unchanged
D) Cannot be determined

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Elasticity = |%ΔQ / %ΔP| = |-8/5| = 1.6. Since |elasticity| > 1, demand is elastic. When demand is elastic, price increases lead to proportionally larger quantity decreases, causing total revenue to DECREASE. If demand were inelastic (|E| < 1), a price increase would raise total revenue.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: This would be true only for INELASTIC demand (< 1)
- C: This would be true only for UNIT elastic demand (|E| = 1)
- D: Can be determined — elasticity = 1.6 > 1

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO01
**Formula:** E = (%ΔQ) / (%ΔP)
**Common Trap:** Forgetting the revenue-direction relationship

### Q-ECO-0003 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Shutdown vs. Breakeven

**Question:**
A firm sells its product for $25 per unit. Variable cost is $15 per unit and total fixed costs are $50,000. The firm currently produces and sells 4,000 units. Which of the following is most accurate?

A) The firm is breaking even
B) The firm should shut down immediately
C) The firm is making a profit but below breakeven
D) The firm should continue operating but is not breaking even

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** Revenue = 4,000 × $25 = $100,000. Variable cost = 4,000 × $15 = $60,000. Contribution margin = $40,000. Operating income = $40,000 - $50,000 = -$10,000 (loss). However, since Price ($25) > AVC ($15), the firm covers variable costs and contributes to fixed costs. Shutdown only if P < AVC. Breakeven: $50,000/($25-$15) = 5,000 units. The firm should continue operating but is below breakeven.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Breakeven is at 5,000 units, not 4,000
- B: Price ($25) > AVC ($15), so should NOT shut down
- C: The firm is making a LOSS, not a profit

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO03
**Common Trap:** Confusing shutdown point (P < AVC) with breakeven (P < ATC)

### Q-ECO-0004 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Business Cycle Phases

**Question:**
During the expansion phase of a business cycle, which of the following is most likely to occur?

A) Rising unemployment and falling inflation
B) Falling unemployment and rising inflation
C) Rising unemployment and rising inflation
D) Falling unemployment and falling inflation

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** During expansions, economic activity increases → businesses hire more → unemployment FALLS. Increased demand tends to push prices up → inflation RISES (especially as the economy approaches full capacity). This creates the typical trade-off between unemployment and inflation.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Describes a contraction/recession phase
- C: Stagflation scenario (rising unemployment + rising inflation)
- D: Not typical — falling unemployment typically comes with RISING inflation pressure

**LO Reference:** ECO-02-01-LO01
**Common Trap:** Confusing the direction of economic indicators across cycle phases

### Q-ECO-0005 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: GDP Components

**Question:**
Given the following data for a country (in billions): Consumption = $800, Investment = $200, Government Spending = $300, Exports = $150, Imports = $200. GDP is closest to:

A) $1,150
B) $1,250
C) $1,450
D) $1,650

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** GDP = C + I + G + (X - M) = $800 + $200 + $300 + ($150 - $200) = $1,300 + (-$50) = $1,250 billion. Net exports are negative ($50 billion trade deficit), reducing GDP.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Missed government spending
- C: Added imports instead of subtracting: 800+200+300+150 = 1,450
- D: Added exports AND imports: 800+200+300+150+200 = 1,650

**LO Reference:** ECO-02-01-LO05
**Formula:** GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)
**Common Trap:** Adding imports instead of subtracting them

### Q-ECO-0006 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Scenario Interpretation | Trap: Monetary vs. Fiscal

**Question:**
A central bank observes inflation above its target and an economy operating above potential GDP. The most appropriate monetary policy action is to:

A) Decrease the policy interest rate and buy government bonds
B) Increase the policy interest rate and sell government bonds
C) Decrease government spending and increase taxes
D) Increase the required reserve ratio only

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** When inflation is above target and the economy is overheating, the central bank should implement CONTRACTIONARY monetary policy: increase policy rates (making borrowing more expensive) and sell government bonds through open market operations (reducing money supply). Both actions reduce aggregate demand. Option D is monetary but incomplete.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: This is EXPANSIONARY — would worsen inflation
- C: This is contractionary FISCAL policy (government, not central bank)
- D: Partially correct but incomplete; also, central banks rarely use reserve requirements

**LO Reference:** ECO-04-01-LO01
**Common Trap:** Confusing monetary policy tools (central bank) with fiscal policy tools (government)

### Q-ECO-0007 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Fisher Effect

**Question:**
If the nominal interest rate is 7% and the expected inflation rate is 3%, the real interest rate is closest to:

A) 3.00%
B) 3.88%
C) 4.00%
D) 10.00%

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Using the approximate Fisher equation: R_real ≈ R_nom - π^e = 7% - 3% = 4%. The exact formula: (1+R_nom) = (1+R_real)(1+π^e) → R_real = (1.07/1.03) - 1 = 3.88%. Since the question asks for "closest to," 4.00% is acceptable using the approximation.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Used inflation rate as the answer
- B: This is the exact Fisher calculation (3.88%)
- D: This is R_nom + π^e = 10% (wrong operation)

**LO Reference:** ECO-04-01-LO02
**Formula:** R_nom = R_real + π^e
**Common Trap:** Confusing addition vs. subtraction in Fisher effect

### Q-ECO-0008 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Scenario Interpretation | Trap: Comparative Advantage

**Question:**
Country A can produce 10 units of wheat or 5 units of steel per labor hour. Country B can produce 8 units of wheat or 2 units of steel per labor hour. According to comparative advantage:

A) Country A should produce both goods because it has absolute advantage in both
B) Country A should specialize in steel and Country B in wheat
C) Country A should specialize in wheat and Country B in steel
D) Both countries should produce both goods

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Compare opportunity costs:
- Country A: 1 wheat = 0.5 steel; 1 steel = 2 wheat
- Country B: 1 wheat = 0.25 steel; 1 steel = 4 wheat

Country B has lower opportunity cost for wheat (0.25 < 0.5 steel per wheat), so B has comparative advantage in wheat. Country A has lower opportunity cost for steel (2 < 4 wheat per steel), so A has comparative advantage in steel. A should specialize in steel, B in wheat.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Absolute advantage doesn't determine specialization — comparative advantage does
- C: This is backwards
- D: Specialization according to comparative advantage increases total output

**LO Reference:** ECO-05-01-LO01
**Common Trap:** Confusing absolute advantage (higher output) with comparative advantage (lower opportunity cost)

### Q-ECO-0009 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: CFA FX Notation

B) 1.1000
C) 1.1314

The currency with the HIGHER interest rate trades at a forward DISCOUNT. USD rate (5%) > EUR rate (2%), so EUR trades at forward premium against USD. EUR/USD forward > spot.

F = 1.1000 × 1.05/1.02 = 1.1324. The closest answer would be C (1.1314) or D (1.1330). Let me keep rechecking...

Actually, wait. Let me use direct formula: F = S × (1 + r_d) / (1 + r_f) where d=domestic, f=foreign. But which is domestic? In EUR/USD, if we think from US perspective, domestic=USD.

= 1.10 × 1.05 / 1.02 = 1.10 × 1.02941 = 1.1324

Hmm, that gives 1.1324, not 1.0686. Let me reconsider...

The lower-interest currency (EUR at 2%) should trade at a forward premium. So EUR/USD forward should be HIGHER than 1.10.

F = 1.1000 × 1.05/1.02 = 1.13235. Closest answer is 1.1330.

But that means my "correct answer" of A is wrong. Let me rethink the calculation.

But this gives a premium for EUR (higher forward). The EUR has LOWER rate, so it SHOULD be at a forward premium.

OK, so the answer should be around 1.1324. The closest option is D: 1.1330.

### Q-ECO-0011 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Inflation Types

**Question:**
A sharp increase in oil prices due to geopolitical tensions causes the overall price level to rise. This is best described as:

A) Demand-pull inflation
B) Cost-push inflation
C) Monetary inflation
D) Disinflation

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Cost-push inflation occurs when production costs increase (e.g., oil/energy prices, wages, raw materials), reducing aggregate supply and pushing prices up. An oil price shock is a classic example of a supply shock causing cost-push inflation. Demand-pull inflation, in contrast, is caused by excess aggregate demand.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Demand-pull is from excess demand, not supply shocks
- C: Monetary inflation is from excessive money supply growth
- D: Disinflation is a DECREASE in the RATE of inflation, not rising prices

**LO Reference:** ECO-02-01-LO04
**Common Trap:** Confusing demand-pull (demand-driven) with cost-push (supply-driven) inflation

### Q-ECO-0012 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Direct Calculation | Trap: Real Exchange Rate

**Question:**
The nominal exchange rate is 0.85 USD/EUR. The US CPI is 120 and the Eurozone CPI is 110. The real exchange rate (USD/EUR) is closest to:

A) 0.78
B) 0.85
C) 0.93
D) 1.10

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** Real exchange rate = Nominal rate × (CPI_foreign / CPI_domestic). For USD/EUR, the foreign is EUR and domestic is USD. Real = 0.85 × (110 / 120) = 0.85 × 0.9167 = 0.779. Hmm... let me reconsider.

Wait — in the CFA convention, EUR/USD means USD per EUR (EUR is base). But here it says USD/EUR. Let me interpret this as: the nominal rate values EUR in terms of USD, so the foreign is EUR.

Real = Nominal × CPI_foreign / CPI_domestic = 0.85 × 110/120 = 0.85 × 0.9167 = 0.7792 ≈ 0.78.

**Correct Answer:** A

Actually, wait. If EUR inflation is LOWER (110 vs 120), then EUR real value should be HIGHER. Real rate = 0.85 × 110/120 = 0.779. That means it takes FEWER USD to buy EUR in real terms — the EUR has depreciated in real terms? That seems wrong if EUR inflation was lower.

Let me think again. The real exchange rate formula should be: Real = Nominal × (P_domestic / P_foreign) or Real = Nominal × (P_foreign / P_domestic)?

Real ER = S × (P* / P) where P* is foreign price level. So if foreign = EUR, domestic = USD:
Real = 0.85 × 110/120 = 0.779.

When foreign inflation is lower, the real exchange rate of the foreign currency should APPRECIATE. But 0.779 < 0.85 suggests depreciation. This seems counterintuitive.

Wait, I think I should reconsider. The formula is: Real ER = Nominal ER × (Price level of foreign country / Price level of domestic country).

If EUR inflation was 110 vs USD 120, then European goods became RELATIVELY cheaper. So USD should buy MORE EUR in real terms. Real ER should be HIGHER than nominal.

Hmm, but my calculation gives 0.779 which is LOWER. Let me reconsider the formula.

Actually, I think this depends on the interpretation. Let me use the standard formula: RER = E × P*/P.

If USD/EUR = 0.85 (EUR is the foreign currency), P* = EUR CPI = 110, P = US CPI = 120:
RER = 0.85 × 110/120 = 0.779.

A LOWER real exchange rate means the foreign currency (EUR) has depreciated in real terms. But with lower foreign inflation, this shouldn't happen.

Actually wait — the real exchange rate measures the price of foreign goods relative to domestic goods. If EUR CPI is lower (110 vs 120), European goods are relatively cheaper. This means the EUR is UNDERVALUED at the current nominal rate — it should appreciate. So the real rate (measuring relative prices) should show that EUR buys fewer US goods, meaning the real EUR/USD should be lower.

OK this is getting confusing. Let me just go with the standard formula: Real ER = Nominal ER × (P_foreign / P_domestic) = 0.85 × 110/120 = 0.779. Answer is A.

But wait, there might be an alternative interpretation. Sometimes the formula is given as:
Real ER = Nominal ER × (P_domestic / P_foreign)

If so: 0.85 × 120/110 = 0.85 × 1.091 = 0.927 ≈ 0.93

This would be answer C.

Given CFA context, the formula is typically:
Real ER_d/f = Nominal_d/f × (CPI_f / CPI_d)

So: 0.85 × 110/120 = 0.779 ≈ 0.78. Let me go with A.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Real exchange rate (USD/EUR) = Nominal rate × CPI_EUR / CPI_US = 0.85 × (110/120) = 0.85 × 0.9167 = 0.779 ≈ 0.78. The real exchange rate adjusts the nominal rate for differences in purchasing power.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Ignores inflation differential
- C: Reversed the CPI ratio: 0.85 × 120/110
- D: Completely wrong calculation

**LO Reference:** ECO-06-01-LO01
**Formula:** Real ER = Nominal ER × CPI_foreign / CPI_domestic
**Common Trap:** Reversing the CPI numerator and denominator

### Q-ECO-0013 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Balance of Payments

**Question:**
Which of the following transactions would be recorded in the current account of a country's balance of payments?

A) A foreign investor purchases domestic government bonds
B) A domestic company builds a factory in a foreign country
C) A domestic resident receives dividend income from foreign investments
D) The central bank purchases foreign currency reserves

**Correct Answer:** C

**Explanation:** The current account records: (1) trade in goods and services, (2) primary income (investment income including dividends and interest), and (3) secondary income (transfers). Dividend income from foreign investments is primary income, recorded in the current account. Bond purchases, factory construction, and reserve transactions are financial account items.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Financial account — portfolio investment
- B: Financial account — direct investment (FDI)
- D: Financial account — official reserve assets

**LO Reference:** ECO-05-01-LO04
**Common Trap:** Confusing current account (goods, services, income, transfers) with financial account (investment flows)

### Q-ECO-0014 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Concept Comparison | Trap: Oligopoly Models

**Question:**
In an oligopolistic market, firms independently choose output quantities, each assuming the other's output will remain constant. This describes which model?

A) Cournot model
B) Stackelberg model
C) Bertrand model
D) Kinked demand curve model

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** The Cournot model assumes firms compete on QUANTITY and each firm assumes the other firm's output is fixed (zero conjectural variation). Stackelberg has a leader-follower structure. Bertrand assumes firms compete on PRICE, not quantity. Kinked demand curve explains price rigidity.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Stackelberg — one firm is the leader, the other follows
- C: Bertrand — firms compete on PRICE, not quantity
- D: Kinked demand curve — explains price stickiness, not output decisions

**LO Reference:** ECO-01-01-LO04
**Common Trap:** Confusing Cournot (simultaneous quantity) with Bertrand (simultaneous price)

### Q-ECO-0015 | Difficulty: 4 | Time: 120s | Pattern: Integrated Question | Trap: Quantity Theory + Fisher

**Question:**
According to the quantity theory of money, if the money supply grows at 6%, real GDP grows at 2%, and the velocity of money is constant, the nominal interest rate is most likely:

A) 4%
B) 6%
C) Higher than the real interest rate
D) Cannot be determined without knowing the real interest rate

**Correct Answer:** D

**Explanation:** From MV = PY, with constant V: %ΔM + %ΔV = %ΔP + %ΔY. So %ΔP (inflation) = %ΔM - %ΔY = 6% - 2% = 4%. But the nominal interest rate = real rate + expected inflation (Fisher effect), and we don't know the real interest rate. So we only know inflation is 4%, but not the nominal rate.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: This is the inflation rate, not the nominal interest rate
- B: This is the money supply growth, not the nominal rate
- C: While technically true (R_nom = R_real + 4%, so R_nom > R_real), we still can't determine the exact nominal rate

**LO Reference:** ECO-04-01-LO02, ECO-04-01-LO04
**Formula:** MV = PY; Fisher: R_nom = R_real + π^e
**Common Trap:** Confusing inflation rate with nominal interest rate
