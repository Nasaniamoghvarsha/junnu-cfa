# SESSION 1 — MORNING PAPER (Questions 1 – 90)

## Section A: Ethical & Professional Standards

### Q-MOCK-0001 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Strictest Law Application

**Question:**
Maria Chen, CFA, works as a senior investment analyst in Country A, where local securities law permits analysts to accept personal gifts up to $500 from corporate executives without disclosure. However, Chen's employer imposes a global policy adhering strictly to the CFA Institute Code and Standards. A company Chen covers sends her a luxury watch valued at $350 after she publishes a research report. Which of the following actions is most appropriate for Chen?

A) Accept the gift since it is under Country A's legal limit of $500
B) Refuse the gift or surrender it to her firm because the stricter CFA Standard applies
C) Keep the gift but disclose it to her research supervisor within 30 days

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Under Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law, members must comply with the stricter of applicable laws, rules, or the CFA Institute Code and Standards. Standard I(B) Independence and Objectivity requires analysts to refuse gifts that could reasonably be expected to compromise their independence or objectivity. A $350 luxury watch is not token; therefore, the stricter CFA Standard prevails over Country A's permissive law.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — members cannot follow permissive local law when the CFA Code and Standards impose a stricter requirement.
- C: Incorrect — post-hoc disclosure does not cure the acceptance of a substantial gift that threatens objectivity.

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-01-LO01 (Standard I(A) Knowledge of the Law)

### Q-MOCK-0002 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Insider Trading Mosaic

**Question:**
David Miller, CFA, gathers information on a pharmaceutical company by combining publicly disclosed FDA filing reports with a private conversation he overheard in an airport terminal between two corporate executives discussing an unexpected clinical trial success. Miller immediately publishes a "Strong Buy" recommendation based on this insight. Did Miller violate Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information?

A) No, because he applied the Mosaic Theory using publicly available data
B) Yes, because the overheard executive conversation contained material nonpublic information
C) No, because airport conversations are in the public domain

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Standard II(A) prohibits trading or causing others to trade on material nonpublic information. Overhearing corporate officers discuss unexpected clinical trial success constitutes acquiring material nonpublic information. Adding a material nonpublic insider disclosure invalidates the Mosaic Theory.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — the Mosaic Theory only applies when combining public information with nonmaterial nonpublic information.
- C: Incorrect — overhearing information in a public location does not transform material nonpublic corporate secrets into public knowledge.

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-02-LO01 (Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information)

### Q-MOCK-0003 | Difficulty: 2 | Time: 60s | Pattern: Ethics Scenario | Trap: Soft Dollar Allocation

**Question:**
An investment manager uses client brokerage commissions ("soft dollars") to purchase specialized financial market data terminals and third-party equity research reports used for client portfolios. Is this practice compliant with Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care?

A) Yes, provided the research and data directly assist the manager in investment decision-making for clients
B) No, because soft dollars can only be used to rebate execution commission costs
C) Yes, even if the research is used exclusively for firm corporate management overhead

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Under Standard III(A), soft dollars belong to the client. Using soft commissions to acquire research and brokerage services that directly benefit clients in the investment decision-making process is permissible and compliant.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — soft dollars are specifically intended to purchase research and brokerage services.
- C: Incorrect — using client soft dollars for corporate management overhead (e.g., office rent) violates fiduciary duty.

**LO Reference:** ETH-02-03-LO01 (Standard III(A) Loyalty, Prudence, and Care)

## Section B: Quantitative Methods

### Q-MOCK-0004 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: Compounding Frequency

**Question:**
An investor deposits $10,000 into a savings account offering a nominal annual interest rate of 6.0%, compounded quarterly. What is the total value of the investment at the end of 5 years?

A) $13,000.00
B) $13,468.55
C) $13,488.50

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Quarterly interest rate $r = 6.0\% / 4 = 1.5\% = 0.015$. Number of compounding periods $n = 5 \times 4 = 20$.
Future value formula: $FV = PV \times (1 + r)^n = 10,000 \times (1.015)^{20} = 10,000 \times 1.346855 = \$13,468.55$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — uses simple interest without compounding ($10,000 + 10,000 \times 0.06 \times 5 = \$13,000$).
- C: Incorrect — uses monthly compounding periods ($n = 60$) at $0.5\%$ rate.

**LO Reference:** QUANT-01-01-LO02 (Time Value of Money Compounding)

### Q-MOCK-0005 | Difficulty: 4 | Time: 120s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: Geometric vs. Arithmetic Mean

**Question:**
An equity fund records the following annual returns over four consecutive years: +15%, -10%, +20%, and -5%. What is the geometric mean annual rate of return?

A) +4.22%
B) +4.73%
C) +5.00%

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Growth factors: $1.15$, $0.90$, $1.20$, $0.95$.
Product of factors: $1.15 \times 0.90 \times 1.20 \times 0.95 = 1.1799$.
Geometric Mean: $(1.1799)^{1/4} - 1 = 1.04222 - 1 = +4.22\%$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — math error in root calculation.
- C: Incorrect — this is the simple arithmetic mean ($5.00\%$), which overstates compounded wealth growth.

**LO Reference:** QUANT-01-02-LO03 (Geometric vs Arithmetic Mean Return)

## Section C: Economics

### Q-MOCK-0006 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Conceptual | Trap: Elasticity & Total Revenue

**Question:**
If the price elasticity of demand for a consumer good is -1.5 (elastic), what will happen to total revenue if the firm increases the selling price by 10%?

A) Total revenue will increase by 15%
B) Total revenue will decrease
C) Total revenue will remain unchanged

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** When demand is price elastic ($|E_d| > 1$), the percentage decrease in quantity demanded is larger than the percentage increase in price. Increasing price causes quantity to fall by $15\%$, leading to a net decrease in total revenue.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — assumes price and revenue move in the same direction when demand is elastic.
- C: Incorrect — total revenue remains unchanged only when demand is unit elastic ($|E_d| = 1$).

**LO Reference:** ECON-01-01-LO01 (Price Elasticity of Demand and Revenue)

## Section D: Financial Statement Analysis

### Q-MOCK-0007 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: Preferred Dividend Deduction

**Question:**
Alpha Corporation reports net income of $5,000,000 and has 1,000,000 weighted average common shares outstanding. Alpha has 100,000 cumulative preferred shares paying a dividend of $5.00 per share. What is Alpha's Basic Earnings Per Share (EPS)?

A) $4.50
B) $5.00
C) $5.50

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Basic EPS $= (\text{Net Income} - \text{Preferred Dividends}) / \text{Weighted Average Common Shares}$.
Preferred Dividends $= 100,000 \times \$5.00 = \$500,000$.
Basic EPS $= (\$5,000,000 - \$500,000) / 1,000,000 = \$4.50$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — fails to deduct preferred dividends from net income ($\$5,000,000 / 1,000,000 = \$5.00$).
- C: Incorrect — erroneously adds preferred dividends to net income.

**LO Reference:** FSA-03-01-LO02 (Basic Earnings Per Share Calculation)

## Section E: Corporate Issuers

### Q-MOCK-0008 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: Tax Shield in WACC

**Question:**
A corporation has a target capital structure of 40% debt and 60% common equity. Its pre-tax cost of debt is 7.0%, the marginal corporate tax rate is 25%, and the cost of equity is 12.0%. What is the firm's Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC)?

A) 9.30%
B) 9.70%
C) 10.00%

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** After-tax cost of debt $r_d(1 - t) = 7.0\% \times (1 - 0.25) = 5.25\%$.
WACC $= (0.40 \times 5.25\%) + (0.60 \times 12.0\%) = 2.10\% + 7.20\% = 9.30\%$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — calculation error in weighting.
- C: Incorrect — fails to adjust cost of debt for tax shield ($0.40 \times 7.0\% + 0.60 \times 12.0\% = 10.00\%$).

**LO Reference:** CORP-01-01-LO01 (Weighted Average Cost of Capital WACC)

# SESSION 2 — AFTERNOON PAPER (Questions 91 – 180)

## Section F: Equity Investments

### Q-MOCK-0009 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: DDM Growth Rate

**Question:**
An analyst calculates that a stock will pay an annual dividend of $2.50 next year ($D_1$). The required rate of return on equity is 10.0%, and dividends are expected to grow at a constant rate of 4.0% indefinitely. According to the Gordon Growth Model, what is the intrinsic value of the stock?

A) $41.67
B) $62.50
C) $65.00

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** Gordon Growth Model: $V_0 = D_1 / (r - g)$.
Given $D_1 = \$2.50$, $r = 10.0\% = 0.10$, and $g = 4.0\% = 0.04$.
$V_0 = 2.50 / (0.10 - 0.04) = 2.50 / 0.06 = \$62.50$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — erroneously adds $g$ to $r$ in denominator ($2.50 / 0.14 = \$17.86$).
- C: Incorrect — compounds $D_1$ again by $(1 + g)$ in numerator ($2.50 \times 1.04 / 0.06 = \$65.00$), which is done only when given current dividend $D_0$.

**LO Reference:** EQ-03-01-LO02 (Gordon Growth Dividend Discount Model)

## Section G: Fixed Income

### Q-MOCK-0010 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: Accrued Interest Addition

**Question:**
A 5-year coupon bond has a annual coupon rate of 6.0% (payable annually) and a yield-to-maturity of 5.0%. Exactly 180 days have passed since the last coupon payment (assuming a 360-day year). If the flat price of the bond is $1,043.29, what is the full (dirty) price of the bond?

A) $1,043.29
B) $1,073.29
C) $1,103.29

**Correct Answer:** B

**Explanation:** $\text{Full Price} = \text{Flat Price} + \text{Accrued Interest}$.
Annual Coupon $= 6.0\% \times \$1,000 = \$60.00$.
$\text{Accrued Interest} = \$60.00 \times (180 / 360) = \$30.00$.
$\text{Full Price} = \$1,043.29 + \$30.00 = \$1,073.29$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- A: Incorrect — gives the flat (clean) price without adding accrued interest.
- C: Incorrect — adds the full annual coupon of $60.00 instead of pro-rating for 180 days.

**LO Reference:** FI-02-01-LO03 (Bond Pricing Full vs Flat Price)

## Section H: Derivatives

### Q-MOCK-0011 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Conceptual | Trap: Net Profit vs Gross Payoff

**Question:**
An investor buys a European call option on a stock with an exercise price of $50. At expiration, the underlying stock price is $58. If the option premium paid was $3.00, what is the net profit to the option buyer at expiration?

A) $5.00
B) $8.00
C) $11.00

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Gross Payoff $= \max(0, S_T - X) = \max(0, 58 - 50) = \$8.00$.
Net Profit $= \text{Gross Payoff} - \text{Option Premium Paid} = \$8.00 - \$3.00 = \$5.00$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — gives gross payoff ($8.00) without deducting the $3.00 premium paid.
- C: Incorrect — erroneously adds option premium to gross payoff.

**LO Reference:** DER-01-01-LO02 (Call Option Payoff and Profit Calculation)

## Section I: Alternative Investments

### Q-MOCK-0012 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Conceptual | Trap: Private Equity Hurdle Rates

**Question:**
A private equity fund charges a 2% management fee and a 20% performance fee (carried interest) with an 8% hurdle rate. If the fund earns a gross return of 6% in a given year, how much performance fee will the general partner (GP) collect?

A) 0%
B) 1.2%
C) 2.0%

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** A hurdle rate is the minimum return the fund must achieve before the GP is eligible to receive performance fees (carried interest). Because the gross return of 6% is below the 8% hurdle rate, the GP receives $0.00$ performance fee.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — calculates $20\% \times 6\% = 1.2\%$, ignoring the hurdle rate condition.
- C: Incorrect — confuses management fee with performance fee.

**LO Reference:** ALT-01-01-LO03 (Private Equity Fee Calculation and Hurdle Rates)

## Section J: Portfolio Management

### Q-MOCK-0013 | Difficulty: 3 | Time: 90s | Pattern: Calculation | Trap: Sharpe Ratio Standard Deviation

**Question:**
A portfolio has an expected return of 14.0%, a beta of 1.2, and a total standard deviation of 18.0%. If the risk-free rate of return is 4.0%, what is the Sharpe Ratio of the portfolio?

A) 0.56
B) 0.78
C) 8.33

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:** Sharpe Ratio $= (R_p - R_f) / \sigma_p = (14.0\% - 4.0\%) / 18.0\% = 10.0\% / 18.0\% = 0.5555 \dots \approx 0.56$.

**Wrong Answer Analysis:**
- B: Incorrect — calculation error.
- C: Incorrect — calculates Treynor Ratio using Beta instead of total standard deviation ($10.0\% / 1.2 = 8.33$).

**LO Reference:** PM-02-01-LO01 (Sharpe Ratio Metrics)
